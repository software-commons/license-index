# License interpretation research

The initial research cutoff was 2026-11-22. This is a record of preliminary document inspection, explanatory drafts and review questions. Each interpretation remains provisional pending inspection of captured original material by a separate Codex execution, exact SCES claim and evidence bindings, adjudication and release qualification. ## MIT

**Summary.** The grant permits use, modification, distribution, sublicensing and sale. The notice condition applies to copies or substantial portions. The last paragraph states the disclaimer of warranties and limitation of liability. [OSI rendering, grant, notice and disclaimer paragraphs](https://opensource.org/license/mit).

**Scenario.** A developer redistributing a library in a commercial package should track which material came from that library and retain its existing copyright and permission notices wherever the notice condition applies. How much constitutes a substantial portion depends on the material. The text has no separately enumerated patent clause. Implied patent rights remain unresolved. [OSI rendering](https://opensource.org/license/mit).

**New project application.** The pinned SPDX reference text has a single notice line with `<year>` and `<copyright holders>`. OSI's rendering also shows year and holder fields. For a new grant on material the rights holder authorizes, the development recipe uses the pinned generated plain text, changes only that notice line and preserves the license body. Keep existing copyright and permission notices for third-party material when the notice condition applies. SPDX's separate matching rules address identification. GitHub documents year and holder substitution for its own reference template. [Pinned SPDX text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spdx/license-list-data/31ba1a50e5397e00a304dbadc76531740e89ee48/text/MIT.txt), [OSI rendering](https://opensource.org/license/mit), [SPDX matching guidelines](https://spdx.github.io/spdx-spec/v3.0.1/annexes/license-matching-guidelines-and-templates/#copyright-notice), [GitHub application guidance](https://choosealicense.com/licenses/mit/#how-to-apply-this-license).

**Representation and reuse.** The SPDX 3.29.0 generated plain text is 1,078 bytes with SHA-256 `b05785f9f18e6716bab63424b11454513b9943a222595b70411009202fc592b5`. The decoded `licenseText` field in the pinned detail record matches those bytes. The separate `standardLicenseTemplate` field matches the SPDX matcher, which has different bytes. The generated-data README traces its output to the XML source and publisher. SPDX’s CC0 change covered named project files such as the schema and validator. A later maintainer response distinguishes the project’s code notices from license texts. Keep MIT on controlled retrieval pending representation-specific evidence for bundling the exact generated text. [SPDX generated-data provenance and licensing](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spdx/license-list-data/31ba1a50e5397e00a304dbadc76531740e89ee48/README.md), [pinned detail](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spdx/license-list-data/31ba1a50e5397e00a304dbadc76531740e89ee48/json/details/MIT.json), [SPDX CC0 change](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-XML/pull/710/files), [SPDX maintainer response](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-XML/issues/2216#issuecomment-1775637696).

**Identity and variants.** OSI labels the published text The MIT License and displays SPDX `MIT` with no version number. The pinned SPDX detail links to OSI. After folding whitespace and normalizing typographic double quotes, the operative grant, condition and disclaimer match the tagged Expat `COPYING` text. Expat’s actual copyright notices remain separate from that comparison. Compare variants by operative text and project grant. MIT-0 omits the notice condition, and X11 adds a restriction on promotional use of the copyright holders’ names. The authorship and original publication date of the exact modern wording remain unresolved. [OSI rendering](https://opensource.org/license/mit), [pinned SPDX detail](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spdx/license-list-data/31ba1a50e5397e00a304dbadc76531740e89ee48/json/details/MIT.json), [Expat `COPYING`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/libexpat/libexpat/R_1_95_8/expat/COPYING), [SPDX MIT-0](https://spdx.org/licenses/MIT-0.html), [SPDX X11](https://spdx.org/licenses/X11.html).

**Approval evidence.** As observed on 2026-11-23, the OSI page displays an approval badge and its API returns `approved: false` with empty approval-date and minutes fields. The same API returns `false` for GPLv3, whose linked minutes record a unanimous approval vote. This shows the flag alone is insufficient evidence of nonapproval. An OSI executive director’s August 2000 message listed the MIT License by name as approved at that time. The exact text attached to that historical listing and the original approval date remain unresolved. Keep approval history separate from the modern text comparison and leave the conflicting metadata provisional pending claim-bound review. ## Apache License 2.0

**Summary.** Section 2 covers reproduction, preparation of derivative works, public display and performance, sublicensing and distribution in Source or Object form, subject to the license. Section 3 limits its patent grant to licensable claims necessarily infringed by a contributor’s contribution alone or in combination with the Work to which it was submitted. Section 6 withholds permission to use the Licensor’s trade names, trademarks, service marks and product names, subject to the stated origin-description and NOTICE exception. Section 7 discusses the Work and Contributions supplied as-is. Section 8 separately addresses contributor liability. Both sections have applicable-law and written-agreement qualifications. [ASF instrument, §§1–9](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0).

**Scenario.** For a changed binary, identify the Work, any Derivative Work and the materials actually shipped. Check the license copy for recipients under §4(a), and check that modified files carry prominent change notices under §4(b). The application of §4(b) to how a specific binary package carries the notices requires separate review. Section 4(c) requires retention of pertinent upstream Source notices in distributed derivative Source. If the Work was distributed with a NOTICE file, §4(d) requires readable pertinent attribution in the distributed Derivative Work. Its location options are a distributed NOTICE file, accompanying Source or documentation, or a generated display in the customary location for third-party notices. [ASF instrument, §§1 and 4](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0).

**Patent termination.** Section 3 applies when the licensee institutes patent litigation against any entity alleging that the work or an incorporated contribution directly or contributorily infringes a patent. Cross-claims and counterclaims can trigger it. The effect stated there is termination of the §3 patent licenses for that work on the filing date. Other conduct and other grants require separate analysis. [ASF instrument, §3](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0).

**New project application.** The ASF's own-software FAQ calls for including a license copy and considering a NOTICE file. It gives a separate source-file notice and a shorter copyright and SPDX identifier alternative. When using the full source-file notice, replace the sample copyright fields in that separate notice. The official license copy keeps the appendix intact, and the ASF expressly permits unchanged reuse of the license. The ASF has a separate notice and header policy for its own projects. The SPDX generated-text representation has different bytes from the ASF plain-text file. [ASF application and reuse guidance](https://www.apache.org/foundation/license-faq.html), [ASF project notice policy](https://www.apache.org/legal/src-headers.html), [pinned ASF plain text](https://github.com/apache/www-site/blob/01b1be9fbc5cd93b6794f5653a58b9b863807f84/content/licenses/LICENSE-2.0.txt), [pinned SPDX text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spdx/license-list-data/31ba1a50e5397e00a304dbadc76531740e89ee48/text/Apache-2.0.txt).

The 2026-11-23 inspection matched the ASF text to the repository's unchanged installation input at 11,358 bytes and SHA-256 `cfc7749b96f63bd31c3c42b5c471bf756814053e847c10f3eb003417bc523d30`. In that exact representation, the §3 grant and litigation-trigger ranges are `[3920, 4553)` and `[4553, 4955)` in bytes. Separate research and independent Codex verification supported six exact claims about this edition. The remaining interpretations and application scenarios in this profile are provisional. [Apache claim-level qualification](apache-20-qualification.md), [pinned ASF plain text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apache/www-site/01b1be9fbc5cd93b6794f5653a58b9b863807f84/content/licenses/LICENSE-2.0.txt).

## GNU GPL 1.0

**Version choice.** Paragraph 7 governs election of a GNU GPL edition under an applicable grant. The two current SPDX identifiers describe version 1 only and version 1 or later. Both refer to the same document. The appendix illustrates the later-version notice, and the copyright holder’s actual notice controls the choice for the program. Paragraph 7 separately addresses an applicable GPL grant with no version number. [GNU GPLv1 paragraphs 0 and 7 and appendix](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/gpl-1.0.txt), [SPDX GPL-1.0-only](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-1.0-only.html), [SPDX GPL-1.0-or-later](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-1.0-or-later.html).

**Binary redistribution.** Paragraph 3(c) covers passing on received source-location information for a noncommercial redistribution when the Program was received solely in object or executable form. Check its actual terms and the other routes in paragraph 3, including the accompanying-source option and the written offer valid for at least three years for any third party. The retained 12,527-byte GNU publisher copy has SHA-256 `49c007bafb5ad6dd9f9d4dae4d3ac0461975498150c05d684c551f0914ab486c`. It is the publisher’s current rendering of the 1989 edition. The pinned SPDX simple-text rendering uses different bytes. [GNU publisher text](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/gpl-1.0.txt), [pinned SPDX text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spdx/license-list-data/31ba1a50e5397e00a304dbadc76531740e89ee48/text/GPL-1.0-only.txt).

## GNU GPL 2.0 and 3.0

**Version 2 summary.** Sections 1 and 2 address notice obligations and conditions on distributing source and changed works. For executable or object-code distribution, §3(a) covers accompanying corresponding source. Section 3(b) covers an accompanying written offer valid for at least three years, available to any third party, at up to the distributor’s physical source-distribution cost. Section 3(c) limits passing along that offer information to noncommercial redistribution after receiving the executable or object code with a compliant §3(b) offer. The final paragraph of §3 also counts equivalent access to copy the source from the same place when the binary is offered for copying at a designated place. Section 4 voids unauthorized copying, modification, sublicensing or distribution, automatically terminates the actor’s rights and preserves the licenses of downstream recipients who remain in full compliance. [GNU GPLv2 §§1–4](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/old-licenses/gpl-2.0.txt).

**Version 2 application and version choice.** Read the authorized grant for the relevant project release and files. The shared GPLv2 document supplies the version 2 terms for both SPDX applications. An explicit version 2 only grant restricts the choice to that version. Section 9 permits electing a later FSF version when the grant specifies the later-version option. Its separate rule allows a recipient to choose any FSF GPL version for a program already covered by a GNU GPL grant that omits the version number. The retained GPLv2 plaintext is 17,984 bytes with SHA-256 `edaef632cbb643e4e7a221717a6c441a4c1a7c918e6e4d56debc3d8739b233f6`. The publisher’s document and application guidance were reopened for this revision. The interpretations await independent SCES verification. [GNU GPLv2 §§0 and 9 and application appendix](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/old-licenses/gpl-2.0.txt), [GNU application guidance](https://gnu.org/licenses/gpl-howto.html), [SPDX version 2 only](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-2.0-only.html), [SPDX version 2 or later](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-2.0-or-later.html).

**Version 3 summary.** Sections 0 and 2 distinguish making, running or propagating without conveyance while the license remains in force from conveyance subject to its terms. Section 2 has a separate permission for qualifying exclusive outsourced work. Sections 4–6 cover conveyance of verbatim source, modified source and object code. Section 5 distinguishes the covered work as a whole from an aggregate of separate, independent works that meet its conditions. Section 8 handles reinstatement per copyright holder. The §11 essential patent claims definition excludes claims infringed only as a consequence of further modification. [GNU GPLv3 §§0, 2, 4–6, 8 and 11](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.txt).

**Scenario.** For a device shipped in binary form under version 3, identify the machine-readable Corresponding Source for the conveyed work. Assess physical delivery or written offer, pass-through offer, network access, and peer-to-peer routes under their respective §6 conditions. Installation Information has a separate User Product trigger involving transfer of possession and use, including a fixed term. The exception applies when neither the conveyor nor any third party retains the ability to install modified object code on that product. For hosted services, distinguish §0’s network interaction without transfer of a copy from actual distributions such as client downloads. Check §13 separately for a qualifying GPLv3/AGPLv3 combination. [GNU GPLv3 §§0, 1, 6 and 13](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.txt).

**Application and version election.** GNU's application guidance calls for a license copy and project notices that establish the version terms. The GPLv3 appendix illustrates a version 3 or later declaration. A project choosing version 3 only needs the declaration appropriate to that choice alongside the same verbatim license document. Section 14 grants the election of a later FSF version when the declaration specifies that option. A versionless declaration and a proxy authorization are separate §14 cases. The common version 3 terms apply when the recipient follows version 3. Mixed project notices require checking the declaration for each relevant component. [GNU application instructions](https://gnu.org/licenses/gpl-howto.html), [GNU GPLv3 §14 and appendix](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.txt), [SPDX GPL-3.0-only](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-3.0-only.html), [SPDX GPL-3.0-or-later](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-3.0-or-later.html).

## GNU LGPL 2.0, 2.1 and 3.0

- **LGPL 2.0 and 2.1.** Inspect each edition's §§2, 4, 5 and 6 separately for library modifications, binary redistribution of the library, works using the library and conditions for distributing a linked combination. Version 2.0 §6(a)–(d) describes routes for providing source and relinking materials or verifying prior delivery. Version 2.1 §6(b) separately describes a suitable shared-library mechanism. It must use the library already present on the user's system at runtime and work with an installed, interface-compatible modified library. Both editions also specify notices and terms for user modification and reverse engineering for debugging those modifications. [GNU Library GPL 2.0](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/lgpl-2.0.txt), [GNU Lesser GPL 2.1](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/lgpl-2.1.txt).
- **LGPL 3.0.** The supplement incorporates GPLv3. Conveying a Combined Work under §4 requires applicable notices, both license documents, and either the stated relinking materials or a suitable shared-library mechanism. Section 4(e) calls for Installation Information only if GPLv3 §6 would otherwise require it, in the amount needed to install and run the modified Combined Work. Section 6 of the supplement treats the later-version option according to the terms specified for the Library as received. [GNU LGPL 3.0 opening, §§0, 4 and 6](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/lgpl-3.0-standalone.html), [GNU GPLv3 §6](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.txt).
- **Scenario.** For an executable linked with an LGPL library, record the edition, packaging, actual distribution terms and whether the recipient can use a changed library. If a Combined Work relies on the LGPLv3 §4(d)(1) mechanism, check that it uses the library already present on the user’s computer at runtime and continues working with a changed, interface-compatible library. Inspect the terms for their effect on modification of the library portions and reverse engineering to debug those changes. For a new LGPL 3.0 grant, GNU’s application guidance calls for both documents and separate licensing notices. [LGPL 2.1 §6 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/LGPL-2.1-only.html), [GNU LGPL 3.0 §4](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/lgpl-3.0-standalone.html), [GNU application guidance](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-howto.html).

## GNU AGPL 3.0

**Summary.** Section 13 adds a specific obligation when a modified version supports remote interaction through a computer network. The modified version must prominently offer those remotely interacting users a way to receive its Corresponding Source at no charge. Sections 4–6 separately address conveying copies. [AGPLv3 §§4–6 and 13 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/AGPL-3.0-only.html), [FSF publisher locator](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html).

**Scenario.** For an operator deploying changed server code, start with whether remote users interact with the modified program, then determine the corresponding material and how those users can obtain it. Examine existing notices and any copies already being conveyed. Whether independently operated components are part of the covered work needs its own inquiry. [AGPLv3 §§0, 1 and 13 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/AGPL-3.0-only.html).

## GNU Free Documentation License 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3

The document’s authorized notice establishes the covered grant, version choice and any Invariant Sections or Cover Texts. For each edition, SPDX supplies two generic identifiers and four alternatives that specify the presence or absence of those features. The six pinned SPDX generated texts within an edition have identical bytes. GNU’s publisher plaintext for that edition is a separate representation with different bytes. The offline index copies the publisher document verbatim under the permission in its opening notice. [GNU FDL 1.1](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/fdl-1.1.txt), [GNU FDL 1.2](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/fdl-1.2.txt), [GNU FDL 1.3](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/fdl-1.3.txt), [SPDX generated-data revision](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/tree/31ba1a50e5397e00a304dbadc76531740e89ee48/text).

- **Copying in quantity.** The Cover Text requirement depends on publication of more than 100 copies in the covered form and on the document’s notice. Version 1.1 addresses printed copies. Versions 1.2 and 1.3 also address media commonly having printed covers. All three separately address publication or distribution of more than 100 Opaque copies. Check the edition’s §1 definition when classifying a file. Versions 1.2 and 1.3 treat editable PDF and PostScript differently from versions produced solely for output. [GNU FDL 1.1 §§1 and 3](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/fdl-1.1.txt), [GNU FDL 1.2 §§1 and 3](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/fdl-1.2.txt), [GNU FDL 1.3 §§1 and 3](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/fdl-1.3.txt).
- **Termination and later editions.** Under versions 1.1 and 1.2, §9 ends rights automatically for the specified acts outside the license and preserves grants for downstream recipients who remain in full compliance. Version 1.3 adds reinstatement rules assessed per copyright holder. They address both absence of timely notice and curing the violation after the holder’s first notice. Version 1.3 §10 also addresses a document-designated proxy’s public acceptance of a future version. [GNU FDL 1.1 §§9–10](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/fdl-1.1.txt), [GNU FDL 1.2 §§9–10](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/fdl-1.2.txt), [GNU FDL 1.3 §§9–10](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/fdl-1.3.txt).
- **Collaboration site scenario.** When researching a claimed historical transition under §11, identify the operator, site, applicable FDL grant and date of republication. Check the earlier incorporation date and the required absence of Cover Texts and Invariant Sections for works first published elsewhere under the license. The provision’s same-site republication window ended before 1 August 2009. [GNU FDL 1.3 §11](https://ftp.gnu.org/pub/gnu/Licenses/fdl-1.3.txt), [GNU FDL 1.3 FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/fdl-1.3-faq.en.html).

## Mozilla Public License 1.1

**Summary.** Source distribution is subject to §3.1, including the license-copy requirement. Sections 3.2 and 3.6 govern source access for executables. Section 3.2 assigns responsibility for source availability to the distributor even when a third party operates the electronic distribution mechanism. Section 3.7 permits a larger product with other code while retaining the obligations for Covered Code. [Mozilla instrument §§3.1–3.7](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/1.1/).

**Scenarios.** A distributor relying on an electronic source link should check the minimum period for that specific Modification and tell executable recipients how it provides the source. The clause distinguishes initial availability from availability of a subsequent version to those recipients. When a package includes a new file containing copied covered material, evaluate §1.9 for that file. A separate file containing only the distributor's independent code does not become a Modification simply by being distributed alongside Covered Code. [Mozilla instrument §§1.7, 1.9, 3.2, 3.6–3.7](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/1.1/).

**Review boundary.** Test the source period against §3.2 rather than inheriting the timing from another edition. Check the actual Exhibit A notice and §§6 and 13 before evaluating a version choice or an alternative license. Mozilla labels the 1.1 FAQ as historical. These notes use the 1.1 instrument for the operative terms. [Mozilla 1.1 legal text](https://www.mozilla.org/media/MPL/1.1/index.0c5913925d40.txt), [historical publisher FAQ](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/1.1/FAQ/).

## Mozilla Public License 2.0

**Summary.** Section 1.10 includes changes to files containing Covered Software and new source files that contain Covered Software. Sections 3.1 and 3.2 distinguish licensing and notification for distributed covered source from source access for executable recipients. Section 3.3 permits distribution of a Larger Work under chosen terms subject to MPL compliance for the covered portion. Its separate Secondary License mechanism requires a qualifying combination and compatible Covered Software. Section 3.4 protects the substance of source notices with a limited factual correction exception. Section 5.2’s specified patent litigation terminates both §2.1 grants for Covered Software. Section 5.1 sets distinct termination and reinstatement terms for noncompliance. [Mozilla instrument §§1–5](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/).

**Scenario.** For an executable distribution that includes a modified MPL file, check how the recipient can obtain the Covered Software's source in a timely manner and at no more than distribution cost. Identify any newly created source files containing Covered Software and evaluate separate material under the Larger Work definition. Applying the license to one's own files calls for examining the distinct attachment and placement guidance in Exhibit A and the FAQ. [Mozilla instrument §§1.7, 1.10, 3 and Exhibit A](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/), [Mozilla MPL FAQ](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/FAQ/).

For patent termination, test §5.2 against claims outside its Contributor Version trigger and against its exclusions for declaratory judgment actions, counterclaims and cross-claims. Keep that trigger separate from noncompliance and reinstatement under §5.1. [Mozilla §§5.1–5.3](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/).

**Exhibit B and Secondary Licenses.** Section 1.5 distinguishes an initial Contributor’s attachment of Exhibit B from prior availability under MPL 1.1 or earlier without a Secondary License. The SPDX no-copyleft-exception identifier describes the Exhibit B application. Section 10.4 requires attaching Exhibit B when distributing incompatible Source Code Form under MPL 2.0. Section 3.3's general Larger Work permission continues to apply subject to the MPL terms for Covered Software. Its second paragraph allows the additional Secondary License route only for a qualifying combination with eligible Covered Software. Test an existing GPLv3 component with each set of source notices and licensing history, and check which grants apply to each part. [Mozilla §§1.5, 1.12, 3.3 and 10.4](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/), [Mozilla FAQ Q14 and Q25](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/FAQ/), [SPDX Exhibit B entry](https://spdx.org/licenses/MPL-2.0-no-copyleft-exception.html).

## Common Public License and Eclipse Public License

- **CPL 1.0.** Eclipse’s publisher note records IBM’s transfer of stewardship and identifies the EPL as the follow-on. Section 7 ends the patent licenses a Contributor granted to a Recipient when that Recipient files against the Contributor over a patent applicable to software. It separately ends the Recipient’s §2(b) rights when the Recipient files against any entity alleging that the Program itself infringes the Recipient’s patents. The Program trigger excludes combinations with other software or hardware. Check the grant, defendant and allegations because a filing can meet both triggers. ## Terms and review questions

- **Copyleft** refers here to license terms requiring specified covered material to be available under specified terms at a defined trigger. **Source distribution** means providing or making available the editable form according to the applicable text and its conditions. Check the trigger, covered material and recipient class in the specific edition.
- **Derivative work** depends on the instrument's definitions and applicable law. **Sublicensing** means permission to grant another party specified rights under the terms of an applicable grant. Both require precise treatment of what rights and material are in scope.
- **Patent grant** means permission for defined patent claims from a party able to grant them. **Attribution** means identifying creators, holders or other specified contributors as required. A **disclaimer** qualifies assurances or liabilities under the document and applicable law. **Termination** ends specified rights when the instrument's conditions are met, sometimes with cure or reinstatement.
- **Conveyance** in GPLv3 has a specific §0 definition involving copies. Elsewhere **distribution** may have a different defined trigger. Distinguish a private change, network interaction, delivery of a copy and shipment of a device. [GPLv3 §§0–1 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-3.0-only.html), [AGPLv3 §13 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/AGPL-3.0-only.html).

Independent review should challenge whether the draft accidentally treats silence as permission, turns an edition-specific condition into a family rule, assumes a combined work where separate material is possible, or claims a source or rights provenance stronger than the captured record. Targeted cases include GPLv3 User Product treatment, AGPL network triggering, limited contributor patent scope and litigation effects, EPL secondary licensing, CDDL version-specific termination and whether FSL or FCL applies to a downstream hosted offering. For MPL 1.1, compare delivery of modified source on the executable media with electronic access through a third party. For PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0, challenge interpretations that equate no charge with a permitted purpose or treat an institution's funding as disqualifying its use. Hold conclusions about compatibility, project compliance, rights clearance and applicable jurisdictions pending their own scoped research.
