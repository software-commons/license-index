# License interpretation research

The research cutoff was 2026-11-22. This is a record of preliminary document inspection, explanatory drafts and review questions. Each interpretation remains provisional pending inspection of captured original material by a separate Codex execution, exact SCES claim and evidence bindings, adjudication and release qualification. A browser-rendered page establishes what was available for inspection. A preserved source artifact requires its own provenance, byte digest and rights decision.

## MIT

**Summary.** The grant permits use, modification, distribution, sublicensing and sale. The notice condition applies to copies or substantial portions. The last paragraph disclaims warranties and limits liability in the terms stated there. [OSI approved text, grant, notice and disclaimer paragraphs](https://opensource.org/license/mit).

**Scenario.** A developer redistributing a library in a commercial package should track which material came from that library and retain its existing copyright and permission notices wherever the notice condition applies. How much constitutes a substantial portion depends on the material. The text has no separately enumerated patent clause. That textual observation leaves the question of implied patent rights unresolved. [OSI approved text](https://opensource.org/license/mit).

**New project application.** The pinned SPDX reference text has a single notice line with `<year>` and `<copyright holders>`. OSI's approved rendering also identifies year and holder as the variables in the notice. A proposed recipe binds the SPDX input digest, permits only these two substitutions in the identified notice line, and preserves the license body. Receiving an existing third-party MIT work does not authorize replacing its notice. [Pinned SPDX text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spdx/license-list-data/31ba1a50e5397e00a304dbadc76531740e89ee48/text/MIT.txt), [OSI approved text](https://opensource.org/license/mit).

## Apache License 2.0

**Summary.** Section 2 covers reproduction, preparation of derivative works, public display and performance, sublicensing and distribution in source or object form, subject to the license. Section 3 provides a patent grant limited to qualifying contributor claims and identifies litigation that ends the patent license for the work. Section 6 reserves trademark permissions with a limited exception relating to origin descriptions and reproduction of NOTICE content. Sections 7 and 8 address warranties and liability. [ASF instrument, §§1–9](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0).

**Scenario.** Someone shipping a changed binary should inspect §4 for the license-copy requirement, the prominent notice in modified files, the rules for pertinent notices in distributed source, and the readable attribution mechanism when the original distribution includes NOTICE and the redistribution is of a derivative work. Section 4(d) gives alternate locations for the readable attribution. A repository's source notice and the contents of a shipped binary package require separate inspection. [ASF instrument, §§1 and 4](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0).

**Patent termination.** Section 3 applies when the licensee institutes patent litigation against any entity alleging that the work or an incorporated contribution directly or contributorily infringes a patent. Cross-claims and counterclaims can trigger it. The effect stated there is termination of the §3 patent licenses for that work on the filing date. Other conduct and other grants require separate analysis. [ASF instrument, §3](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0).

**New project application.** The ASF's guidance calls for a license copy and discusses a separate copyright or SPDX source header. The appendix in the official `LICENSE` is preserved verbatim alongside the rest of the document. Its sample copyright fields are replaced in a separate source-file notice when that approach is used. ASF expressly permits unchanged reuse. The SPDX generated-text presentation has different formatting from the ASF plain-text file and needs a distinct source label. [ASF application and reuse guidance](https://www.apache.org/foundation/license-faq.html), [ASF plain text](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0.txt), [ASF website source](https://github.com/apache/www-site/blob/main/content/licenses/LICENSE-2.0.txt), [pinned SPDX text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spdx/license-list-data/31ba1a50e5397e00a304dbadc76531740e89ee48/text/Apache-2.0.txt).

## GNU GPL 2.0 and 3.0

**Version 2 summary.** Sections 1 and 2 address source copying, distribution and changed works, including notice obligations and conditions on distributed works based on the Program. Section 3 provides alternatives for supplying corresponding source when distributing object code. Section 4 addresses termination for activities outside the license. Section 9 lets recipients choose a later FSF version when the Program was licensed using the specified later-version option. The two SPDX references identify the application choice. A copy of the general GPLv2 terms does not distinguish `GPL-2.0-only` from `GPL-2.0-or-later` in an actual project. Inspect the application notices or another authoritative grant. [GPLv2 §§1–4 and 9 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-2.0-only.html), [SPDX or-later reference](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-2.0-or-later.html), [FSF publisher locator](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/old-licenses/gpl-2.0.en.html).

**Version 3 summary.** Sections 0 and 2 distinguish running or making a private modification from conveying copies. Sections 4–6 cover conveying verbatim source, modified source and non-source forms. Non-source conveyance requires use of an applicable mechanism for Corresponding Source, and some User Product transactions also require Installation Information. Section 8 addresses termination and possible reinstatement, §11 defines contributor patent provisions and §14 addresses later versions. [GPLv3 §§0, 2, 4–6, 8, 11 and 14 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-3.0-only.html), [FSF publisher locator](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html).

**Scenario.** For a device shipped in binary form, compare the actual licensing version and the transaction with the enumerated source mechanisms. For GPLv3 establish whether §6's User Product and possession/use transfer conditions apply before concluding Installation Information is required. GPLv3 §0 distinguishes network interaction without transfer of a copy from conveyance. Distribution of a client download or an appliance image is a separate fact to investigate. [GPLv2 §3 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-2.0-only.html), [GPLv3 §§0 and 6 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-3.0-only.html).

**Application and acquisition limits.** GNU's application guidance calls for a copy of the license and project notices that identify applicable versions. Copy the publisher's verbatim license document and pair it with the correct application notices outside the instrument. The research browser's direct calls to GNU returned fetch timeouts, so SPDX's exact edition pages were opened for the initial clause inspection. GNU's publisher-hosted GPLv2 and GPLv3 plain text was subsequently retrieved, fingerprinted, and bundled verbatim. Independent SCES verification of scoped GPL interpretations remains pending. [FSF application instructions](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-howto.en.html), [publisher text manifest](../../legal-texts/manifest.json).

## GNU LGPL 2.0, 2.1 and 3.0

- **LGPL 2.0 and 2.1.** Inspect each edition's §§2, 4, 5 and 6 separately for library modifications, binary redistribution of the library, works using the library and conditions for distributing a linked combination. §6 includes ways to enable users to use a modified library. LGPL 2.1 §6 includes a suitable shared-library mechanism as one option subject to its actual conditions. BSD-style notice retention alone would omit the linked-work conditions. [LGPL 2.0 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/LGPL-2.0-only.html), [LGPL 2.1 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/LGPL-2.1-only.html), [FSF LGPL 2.1 publisher locator](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/old-licenses/lgpl-2.1.en.html).
- **LGPL 3.0.** The instrument supplements and incorporates GPLv3. §4 lays out additional permission for conveying a Combined Work, including notice and license copies, and conditional options involving relinking materials or a suitable shared-library mechanism. §4(e) addresses Installation Information where required by GPLv3 and needed to install and execute a modified Combined Work. [LGPL 3.0 §§0 and 4 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/LGPL-3.0-only.html), [GPLv3 §6 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-3.0-only.html).
- **Scenario.** For an executable that includes an LGPL library, record the edition, how the executable and library are packaged, whether a changed library can be used, whether required notices and texts are present, and what the EULA says about modification and reverse engineering. A label such as “dynamically linked” alone leaves material facts unresolved. A one-document LGPL 3.0 install leaves its incorporated GPLv3 and application notices unaddressed. [LGPL 2.1 §6 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/LGPL-2.1-only.html), [LGPL 3.0 §4 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/LGPL-3.0-only.html), [FSF GNU application instructions](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-howto.en.html).

## GNU AGPL 3.0

**Summary.** Section 13 adds a specific obligation when a modified version supports remote interaction through a computer network. The modified version must prominently offer those remotely interacting users a way to receive its Corresponding Source at no charge. Sections 4–6 separately address conveying copies. [AGPLv3 §§4–6 and 13 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/AGPL-3.0-only.html), [FSF publisher locator](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html).

**Scenario.** For an operator deploying changed server code, start with whether remote users interact with the modified program, then determine the corresponding material and how those users can obtain it. Examine existing notices and any copies already being conveyed. Whether independently operated components are part of the covered work needs its own inquiry. [AGPLv3 §§0, 1 and 13 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/AGPL-3.0-only.html).

## Mozilla Public License 1.1

**Summary.** Source distribution is subject to §3.1, including the license-copy requirement. Sections 3.2 and 3.6 govern source access for executables. Section 3.2 assigns responsibility for source availability to the distributor even when a third party operates the electronic distribution mechanism. Section 3.7 permits a larger product with other code while retaining the obligations for Covered Code. [Mozilla instrument §§3.1–3.7](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/1.1/).

**Scenarios.** A distributor relying on an electronic source link should check the minimum period for that specific Modification and tell executable recipients how it provides the source. The clause distinguishes initial availability from availability of a subsequent version to those recipients. When a package includes a new file containing copied covered material, evaluate §1.9 for that file. A separate file containing only the distributor's independent code does not become a Modification simply by being distributed alongside Covered Code. [Mozilla instrument §§1.7, 1.9, 3.2, 3.6–3.7](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/1.1/).

**Review boundary.** Test the source period against §3.2 rather than inheriting the timing from another edition. Check the actual Exhibit A notice and §§6 and 13 before evaluating a version choice or an alternative license. Mozilla labels the 1.1 FAQ as historical. These notes use the 1.1 instrument for the operative terms. [Mozilla 1.1 legal text](https://www.mozilla.org/media/MPL/1.1/index.0c5913925d40.txt), [historical publisher FAQ](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/1.1/FAQ/).

## Mozilla Public License 2.0

**Summary.** Section 1.10 includes changes to files containing Covered Software and new source files that contain Covered Software. Sections 3.1 and 3.2 require source licensing and access for the Covered Software when distributed in the specified forms. Section 3.3 permits distribution of a Larger Work under chosen terms while keeping MPL requirements for the covered portion, with a conditional Secondary License mechanism. Section 3.4 protects notices. Sections 2 and 5 describe grants and termination, including specific patent provisions. [Mozilla instrument §§1–5](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/).

**Scenario.** For a closed distribution that includes a modified MPL file, plan how binary recipients will obtain the Covered Software's source. Identify any newly created source files containing MPL material. Separately check which other files are distinct material under the instrument's Larger Work definition. Mozilla's Exhibit A notice and guidance show that copying the license text is only one of the project application tasks. [Mozilla instrument §§1.7, 1.10, 3 and Exhibit A](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/), [Mozilla MPL FAQ](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/FAQ/).

## Eclipse Public License 1.0 and 2.0

- **EPL 1.0.** Section 2 lists copyright and limited contributor-patent grants. Section 3 permits object-code distribution under another agreement subject to specified terms. Those terms include a statement that the Program's source is available from the distributor and information on how to obtain it. Source distribution must follow EPL 1.0 and include a copy of the agreement. The definition of Contribution has a separate-module exclusion with two conditions. [Eclipse EPL 1.0, §§1–3](https://www.eclipse.org/legal/epl/epl-v10.html).
- **EPL 2.0.** Sections 1 and 3 use the edition's own Program and Modified Works definitions. A distributor makes the Program available as source and tells recipients how to obtain it. Source is subject to EPL 2.0 or the §3.2(a) Secondary License route where its separate-file combination and initial Contributor notice conditions hold. Sections 3.3 and 4 add notice and commercial distribution considerations. [Eclipse EPL 2.0, §§1–4 and Exhibit A](https://www.eclipse.org/org/documents/epl-2.0/EPL-2.0.pdf), [publisher FAQ](https://www.eclipse.org/legal/epl-2.0/faq/).
- **Scenario.** Inspect the initial contributor's notices and the source file boundaries of a proposed secondary-license distribution. An `EPL-2.0` identifier by itself does not establish that the source carries the Exhibit A notice. Check a binary commercial offering separately for the §4 scope and the claims or acts that trigger its defense and indemnity provisions. [Eclipse EPL 2.0, §§3–4 and Exhibit A](https://www.eclipse.org/org/documents/epl-2.0/EPL-2.0.pdf).

## CDDL 1.0 and 1.1

**Summary.** In both inspected editions, §§1 and 3 attach conditions to Covered Software, including source availability and notices. Distribution of Covered Software in executable form triggers an obligation to make its source available under the CDDL and communicate how recipients can obtain it. The text permits different terms for an executable subject to protecting source rights. Sections 2 and 6 govern the distinct patent and termination rules. The applicable edition must be tracked because the exact grant, steward and termination wording matters. [CDDL 1.0, §§1–3 and 6 via OSI](https://opensource.org/license/CDDL-1.0), [CDDL 1.1, §§1–3 and 6 via OSI](https://opensource.org/license/CDDL-1.1).

**Scenario.** A package combining CDDL components with other code requires a source availability plan for the Covered Software. Review the precise definition of Modifications and the Larger Work clause, the source license copy and the existing notices. A licensing or compliance verdict for an actual combination remains out of scope. [CDDL 1.0 §§1 and 3 via OSI](https://opensource.org/license/CDDL-1.0), [CDDL 1.1 §§1 and 3 via OSI](https://opensource.org/license/CDDL-1.1).

## Public-domain tools, documentation and data

- **`CC0-1.0`.** Creative Commons' dedication uses a waiver of listed Copyright and Related Rights to the extent permitted, a fallback license where the waiver is ineffective, and limitations. §4 expressly excludes affecting the affirmer's patent and trademark rights. Verify who controls the rights and what material the dedication covers. Creative Commons' software guidance discusses notices and a plain-text COPYING file. [CC0 legal code §§1–4](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en), [CC0 software and application guidance](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/CC0_FAQ).
- **`CC-PDM-1.0`.** The Public Domain Mark identifies material believed to be free of known copyright restrictions worldwide. It is a designation. Using a mark and dedicating rights through CC0 are distinct operations. Record the marking purpose and territorial limitations independently of the SPDX crosswalk. [Creative Commons Public Domain Mark guidance](https://creativecommons.org/public-domain/pdm/), [SPDX entry](https://spdx.org/licenses/CC-PDM-1.0.html).
- **`CC-BY-NC-4.0`.** The grant in §2(a) ties reproduction and sharing to NonCommercial purposes. §§1, 3 and 4 define the restriction, attribution obligations and database-rights handling. For a software manual, separately identify the licensed manual, the licensing of any code samples, the purpose of the intended use and the applicable attribution details. Creative Commons distinguishes software from accompanying documentation in its guidance. [CC BY-NC legal code §§1–4](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/legalcode.en), [Creative Commons software guidance](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#can-i-apply-a-creative-commons-license-to-software).
- **`ODbL-1.0`.** The publisher distinguishes database rights from rights in individual contents. The conditions differ for public use of the database, a derivative database and a produced work. For an application using a third-party database, identify which material is distributed, which rights are held in the contents, and which form the user receives before interpreting share-alike and access duties. [Open Data Commons legal code §§2–4](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/).

## Source-available instruments and exceptions

- **`BUSL-1.1`.** The Business Source License 1.1 template grants copying, modification, creation of derivative works, redistribution and non-production use. It provides for an optional Additional Use Grant for limited production use. The transition to the Change License is triggered by the earlier of the Change Date or the fourth anniversary of the first publicly available distribution of the specific version under that license. For a deployment, inspect the project-specific parameters and the actual version history. [MariaDB BSL 1.1 parameters and terms](https://mariadb.com/bsl11/).
- **`FSL-1.1-ALv2` and `FSL-1.1-MIT`.** Each template defines qualifying competing use and includes future-license terms. Distinguish edition, first availability of the version under the instrument, and the actual named future license. Internal use and competing commercial products require separate application analysis under the enumerated conditions. [FSL publisher templates](https://fsl.software/), [SPDX ALv2 entry](https://spdx.org/licenses/FSL-1.1-ALv2.html), [SPDX MIT entry](https://spdx.org/licenses/FSL-1.1-MIT.html).
- **`PolyForm-Noncommercial-1.0.0`.** The general copyright grant and the grant for changes or new works refer to permitted purposes. A separate distribution grant covers copies, including permitted changes. Recipients of any part must get the terms or their specified URL and any licensor-provided required notice lines. A university's use may qualify regardless of the funding source under the specified institutional clause, while a for-profit participant's own use must be assessed separately. For a free prototype with an anticipated commercial application, test the personal-use clause and any other permitted-purpose ground before drawing a conclusion. Assess distribution of copies separately from use. [PolyForm instrument, Copyright License, Distribution License, Notices, Noncommercial Purposes, Personal Uses and Noncommercial Organizations](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/noncommercial/1.0.0), [publisher plain text](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/noncommercial/1.0.0.txt).
- **`Classpath-exception-2.0`.** The published exception grants additional permission concerning linking the covered library to independent modules, subject to the exception conditions, including following the terms of those modules. The definition of independence and the applicable project grant need their own inspection. Search can resolve the exception, while installing an expression such as `GPL-2.0-only WITH Classpath-exception-2.0` depends on the actual base license, exception, notices and covered files. [SPDX exception text and source locator](https://spdx.org/licenses/Classpath-exception-2.0.html), [GNU Classpath license locator](https://www.gnu.org/software/classpath/license.html).

## Terms and review questions

- **Copyleft** refers here to license terms requiring specified covered material to be available under specified terms at a defined trigger. **Source distribution** means providing or making available the editable form according to the applicable text and its conditions. Check the trigger, covered material and recipient class in the specific edition.
- **Derivative work** depends on the instrument's definitions and applicable law. **Sublicensing** means permission to grant another party specified rights under the terms of an applicable grant. Both require precise treatment of what rights and material are in scope.
- **Patent grant** means permission for defined patent claims from a party able to grant them. **Attribution** means identifying creators, holders or other specified contributors as required. A **disclaimer** qualifies assurances or liabilities under the document and applicable law. **Termination** ends specified rights when the instrument's conditions are met, sometimes with cure or reinstatement.
- **Conveyance** in GPLv3 has a specific §0 definition involving copies. Elsewhere **distribution** may have a different defined trigger. Distinguish a private change, network interaction, delivery of a copy and shipment of a device. [GPLv3 §§0–1 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/GPL-3.0-only.html), [AGPLv3 §13 via SPDX](https://spdx.org/licenses/AGPL-3.0-only.html).

Independent review should challenge whether the draft accidentally treats silence as permission, turns an edition-specific condition into a family rule, assumes a combined work where separate material is possible, or claims a source or rights provenance stronger than the captured record. Targeted cases include GPLv3 User Product treatment, AGPL network triggering, limited contributor patent scope and litigation effects, EPL secondary licensing, CDDL version-specific termination and whether FSL or FCL applies to a downstream hosted offering. For MPL 1.1, compare delivery of modified source on the executable media with electronic access through a third party. For PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0, challenge interpretations that equate no charge with a permitted purpose or treat an institution's funding as disqualifying its use. Hold conclusions about compatibility, project compliance, rights clearance and applicable jurisdictions pending their own scoped research.
